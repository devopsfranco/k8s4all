- Give me generic solutions or "best practices" yet

## What This Module Accomplishes

By the end of this conversation, you should:

1. **Understand root causes**, not just symptoms
2. **See patterns** across your challenges
3. **Identify priorities** - what matters most
4. **Recognize what you control** vs. what you need to navigate
5. **Have specific examples** that will make future learning concrete

## Why Go Deep on Challenges?

Because **the best learning is problem-driven**.

When you deeply understand:
- What's actually wrong
- Why it's wrong
- What's causing it
- How it affects you

Then concepts, tools, and approaches become **solutions to real problems** instead of abstract ideas.

## Guiding Questions for Yourself

As you converse with the AI, keep these questions in mind:

**About the Challenge Itself:**
- How long has this been a problem?
- Is it getting better, worse, or staying the same?
- When does it hurt the most?
- What's the real cost? (Time? Money? Stress? Opportunity?)

**About Your Role:**
- What part of this challenge is mine to solve?
- What do I need from others?
- What authority or resources do I have?
- What would make this easier for me?

**About Solutions:**
- What's worked before (even partially)?
- What definitely hasn't worked?
- What assumptions am I making?
- What would "good enough" look like (not perfection)?

## Going Deeper: The "Five Whys"

When you describe a challenge, the AI might use a technique called "Five Whys" - asking "why" multiple times to get to root causes.

For example:
- **Challenge**: "Our team meetings waste time"
- **Why?** "Because people aren't prepared"
- **Why?** "Because they don't know what will be discussed"
- **Why?** "Because we don't set clear agendas"
- **Why?** "Because no one owns meeting preparation"
- **Root cause**: Lack of ownership for meeting structure

This helps you move from "meetings are bad" to "we need clear ownership of meeting preparation" - a much more actionable insight.

## Example Conversation Flow

Here's what a good conversation might look like:

**You**: "One of my biggest challenges is that projects take too long to complete"

**AI**: "Let's explore that. When you say 'too long,' what specifically happens? Walk me through a recent project."

**You**: [Describes a specific example]

**AI**: "I notice you mentioned waiting for approvals multiple times. Tell me more about that approval process - who's involved, and what are they approving?"

**You**: [Explains approval bottleneck]

**AI**: "Interesting. It sounds like the delay isn't in the work itself, but in handoffs and approvals. Do other types of projects have similar patterns?"

**You**: [Realizes pattern across multiple projects]

**AI**: "Let's dig into one of those approval steps. What information do approvers need? How do they currently get it?"

And so on - each question helps you understand the challenge more deeply.

## Red Flags: When to Redirect

If your AI conversation feels off track, watch for these signs:

**Too Generic**: AI gives you "10 tips for better meetings" instead of asking about YOUR meetings
- **Redirect**: "I don't want generic tips yet. Can you ask me more about my specific situation?"

**Too Quick**: AI jumps to solutions before understanding the problem
- **Redirect**: "I appreciate the suggestion, but I want to understand the problem better first. Can you ask me more questions?"

**Too Surface**: AI accepts your first explanation without digging deeper
- **Redirect**: "I feel like there's more to this. Can you help me think about why this is really happening?"

**Not Listening**: AI doesn't reference or build on what you've said
- **Redirect**: "Earlier I mentioned [X]. I think that's connected to this. Can we explore that?"
