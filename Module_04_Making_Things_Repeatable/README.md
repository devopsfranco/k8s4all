# Module 04: Making Things Repeatable

## Overview

Success shouldn't require heroics every time. This module helps you create reliable processes and patterns that work consistently, freeing your energy for challenges that truly need it.

## Core Concept

**Ad hoc**: Figure it out each time, results vary, knowledge stays in heads

**Repeatable**: Follow proven patterns, results consistent, knowledge embedded in systems

The goal isn't rigid procedures - it's reliable outcomes with appropriate structure.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will be able to:

1. **Identify what's worth making repeatable** vs. what should stay flexible
2. **Extract patterns** from successful outcomes
3. **Design simple processes** people will actually follow
4. **Capture knowledge** from individual expertise into systematic approaches
5. **Balance structure with flexibility** for different situations
6. **Build continuous improvement** into repeatable work

## Why This Matters

Repeatability enables:

- **Consistent quality**: Outcomes don't depend on luck or who does it
- **Scalability**: Others can succeed without depending on specific experts
- **Efficiency**: No reinventing the wheel or relearning lessons
- **Reliability**: Stakeholders know what to expect
- **Growth**: New people come up to speed faster
- **Innovation**: Energy freed from repetitive problem-solving goes to new challenges

## Key Frameworks

### Pattern Recognition
Identifying what makes success reproducible

### Process Design Principles
- Simple enough to follow
- Structured enough to be reliable
- Flexible enough to adapt
- Visible enough to find
- Improvable over time

### Knowledge Capture
Moving expertise from individuals into systems

### The Right Amount of Process
Too little: chaos and inconsistency
Too much: bureaucracy and rigidity
Just right: reliable outcomes without burden

## Common Applications

Making things repeatable helps with:

- **Onboarding**: Getting new people productive faster
- **Quality control**: Ensuring consistent outcomes
- **Delegation**: Enabling others to handle tasks
- **Training**: Transferring knowledge systematically
- **Scaling**: Growing beyond yourself or your team
- **Improvement**: Building on what works rather than starting over

## Example Transformations

**Before**: "Every project kickoff is different. Sometimes we forget important stakeholders, sometimes we skip critical planning steps. It depends on who runs it."

**After**: "We have a simple project kickoff template: 1) Identify stakeholders, 2) Define success criteria, 3) Map dependencies, 4) Establish communication plan. Takes 90 minutes, ensures we don't miss critical elements. We still adapt it to each project, but the core structure is reliable."

**Before**: "When Sarah handles customer issues, they get resolved quickly. When others try, it takes 3x as long and customers are less happy."

**After**: "We worked with Sarah to extract her approach: 1) Acknowledge within 1 hour, 2) Ask clarifying questions before proposing solutions, 3) Confirm understanding before implementing, 4) Follow up after resolution. Now everyone follows this pattern and customer satisfaction is consistently high."

## Common Pitfalls

**Over-standardizing**: Making rigid what should be flexible
- **Solution**: Ask "does this need to be the same every time?"

**Under-documenting**: Keeping knowledge in heads
- **Solution**: "If I were gone tomorrow, could someone else do this?"

**Too complex**: Creating processes nobody follows
- **Solution**: "What's the simplest version that would work?"

**Set and forget**: Process becomes obsolete
- **Solution**: Build in regular review and update mechanisms

## Why This Works

Repeatability works because:

1. **Cognitive offloading**: Frees mental energy for higher-value thinking
2. **Compound learning**: Improvements benefit all future iterations
3. **Reduces variance**: Eliminates failure modes and inconsistency
4. **Enables delegation**: Others can succeed without constant guidance
5. **Creates foundation**: Can't improve what you can't repeat

## Remember

**Process serves people, not vice versa** - If a process makes work harder, fix the process

**Repeatability ≠ rigidity** - Good processes have appropriate flexibility

**Start simple, evolve** - Better to have a simple process people follow than complex one they ignore

**The best process is invisible** - Flows naturally without conscious thought

**Make doing it right easier than doing it wrong** - Default path should be the good path
