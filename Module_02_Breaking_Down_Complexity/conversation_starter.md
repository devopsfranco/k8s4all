## What This Module Teaches

This isn't about learning theory. It's about learning **practical thinking tools** you can apply to your real challenges.

### Key Frameworks You'll Explore

**Decomposition**
- How to break big problems into smaller ones
- How to find natural boundaries and divisions
- How to map dependencies
- How to identify what you can tackle independently

**Abstraction**
- How to zoom out and see the big picture
- How to identify what really matters vs. what's noise
- How to find patterns across different situations
- How to think in principles, not just details

**Prioritization**
- How to decide what to work on first
- How to assess impact vs. effort
- How to distinguish urgent from important
- How to say no or defer things

**Simplification**
- How to spot unnecessary complexity
- How to make things simpler without losing value
- How to find "good enough" solutions
- How to remove constraints you've created

## Example Conversation Flow

**You**: "I want to work on the challenge we identified about project delays. It feels really complex because there are so many people involved and so many places things get stuck."

**AI**: "Great choice. Let's start with decomposition. Think about this project delay challenge as having different layers. Can you describe the journey a project takes from start to finish in your organization?"

**You**: [Describes the process]

**AI**: "I hear several distinct phases: Planning, Approval, Execution, and Review. Let's map which phase causes the most delays. Where does time get lost most often?"

**You**: [Identifies approval phase]

**AI**: "Now let's decompose the approval phase. What are the distinct steps? Who's involved in each?"

**You**: [Breaks down approval steps]

**AI**: "Looking at what you've described, I see three separate approval types: Technical, Budget, and Stakeholder. They all happen sequentially. What if we abstracted these as 'validation checkpoints'? Do they all need to happen in sequence, or could some happen in parallel?"

**You**: [Has realization about potential parallelization]

And so on - each question helps you see your challenge from a new angle and spot opportunities.

## Thinking Tools You'll Use

### The Layer Cake
Breaking problems into vertical layers (like infrastructure, process, people, culture)

### The Dependency Map
Visualizing what depends on what, finding critical paths

### The Impact/Effort Matrix
Plotting actions by impact vs. effort to prioritize

### The Constraint Analysis
Identifying what's limiting you and whether it's real or assumed

### The 80/20 Rule
Finding the 20% of causes creating 80% of problems

### The "What If" Game
Challenging assumptions by asking "what if this constraint didn't exist?"

## Common Breakthroughs

People often discover:

- "I was treating this as one big problem, but it's actually 3 separate problems"
- "Most of the complexity is optional - we created it ourselves"
- "If I tackle this one piece first, several other pieces become easier"
- "I was overthinking this - there's a much simpler approach"
- "The constraint I thought was fixed is actually negotiable"

## Time Investment

## Tips for Success

**Work with real challenges**: Don't use hypothetical examples

**Draw it out**: Visual diagrams help see complexity (ask your AI to help you create simple text diagrams)

**Question assumptions**: If something seems like it "must" be done a certain way, challenge it

**Iterate**: Your first breakdown won't be perfect. Refine as you learn

**Focus on action**: The goal is progress, not perfect analysis

## Red Flags

Watch out for:

**Analysis paralysis**: Breaking things down is useful, but don't get stuck in endless analysis
- **Redirect**: "This is helpful, but I'm ready to identify where to start. What's the smallest meaningful action I can take?"

**Generic frameworks**: AI gives you textbook frameworks without connecting to YOUR situation
- **Redirect**: "How does this specifically apply to my challenge with [X]?"

**Too abstract**: Conversation stays theoretical without getting concrete
- **Redirect**: "Let's use my actual example. Walk me through applying this to [specific challenge]."

**Missing the forest**: Getting lost in details without seeing the big picture
- **Redirect**: "Let's zoom back out. What's the core issue we're trying to solve here?"
